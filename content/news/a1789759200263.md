body_en: |
  A business card helps someone remember your name, organization, and how to follow up. Practices vary by setting, region, and workplace. Rather than relying on rigid rules about rank, focus on considerate communication, respect for the other person's choice, and information that is accurate and easy to read.

  ## Offering a card: be clear and considerate

  - Choose an appropriate moment, such as an introduction or when someone expresses interest in staying in touch. There is no need to push a card into every conversation.
  - Check that the card is clean and legible. If the other person uses another language, offer a version they can read more easily when available.
  - For a meeting or business event, bring enough cards and keep them somewhere convenient where they are unlikely to be damaged.
  - If someone does not have a card or is not ready to exchange one, continue the conversation politely instead of pressuring them.

  ## Receiving a card: take a moment and keep it safe

  - Accept the card with thanks and take a moment to note the person's name and preferred form of address.
  - Keep it visible during the conversation, then put it away safely afterward so it is not lost among other materials.
  - If you need to note a follow-up, use your own notes or contact system rather than writing on the other person's card.
  - Before reaching out later, use a contact method the person chose to share and respect any communication preferences they expressed.

  ## Card design: make key details easy to read

  Include information that supports the intended contact. Common choices are a name, organization or brand, a relevant role, and one primary business contact method. Add a website or other channel when useful. Avoid crowding the card, making type too small, or letting decoration interfere with readability.

  Before sending a card to print, check names, roles, phone numbers, URLs, and QR codes for accuracy, then review the text at its final size. Test QR codes by scanning them. If a card includes personal contact details, make sure they are appropriate to share publicly.

  ## In short

  There is no single exchange ritual that fits every culture or setting. Be natural, accurate, and respectful, and let the other person choose whether to continue the connection.

商务名片可以帮助新认识的人记住你的姓名、机构和后续联系方法。交换方式会因场合、地区和组织习惯而不同；比起遵守僵化的排名规则，更重要的是礼貌沟通、尊重对方意愿，并让信息准确易读。

## 递出名片：清楚说明并尊重场合

- 选择合适的时机，在自我介绍或对方表达希望保持联系时递出名片，不必在每次谈话中强行推广。
- 递交前确认名片干净、内容清晰；若对方使用不同语言，可选择更便于对方阅读的一版。
- 参加会议或商务活动时，可以准备足够的名片，并放在方便取用、不易折损的位置。
- 如果对方没有名片或暂时不方便交换，礼貌地继续交流即可，不要施压索取。

## 接收名片：认真查看，妥善保存

- 接过名片后可简短致谢，并花一点时间确认姓名和职位的读法或称呼。
- 交谈时将名片放在容易查看的位置；会后再收好，避免随手遗失或与其他资料混在一起。
- 需要记录后续事项时，可在自己的笔记或联系人系统中记录，不要擅自在对方名片上涂写。
- 后续联系前，确认使用的是对方愿意提供的联系方式，并遵守对方表达的沟通边界。

## 名片设计：让重要信息一眼可读

名片上的内容应服务于实际联系需要。通常可优先考虑姓名、机构或品牌、与场景相关的职位，以及一个主要的工作联系方式；网站或其他联系渠道可按需要添加。尽量避免信息过多、字号过小或装饰影响阅读。

交付印刷前，检查姓名、职务、电话号码、网址和二维码等信息是否准确，并在最终尺寸下确认文字清晰。若使用二维码，建议实际扫码测试；如名片包含个人联系方式，也应先确认这些信息适合公开分享。

## 简要总结

名片交换没有适用于所有文化和场合的固定动作。保持自然、准确和尊重，让对方能轻松选择是否继续联系，通常比递送次序或数量更重要。
