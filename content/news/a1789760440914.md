body_en: |
  Good layout is not only about making a page look orderly. It should help readers find key information and move through the content comfortably. Articles, flyers, and event materials have different sizes and reading contexts, so use these principles as a starting point and adapt them to the work.

  ## 1. Create a clear information hierarchy

  Distinguish the main title, section headings, body text, and supporting details. Use size, weight, placement, or spacing to show how they relate. Keep the hierarchy focused: if everything is emphasized, readers cannot tell where to look first.

  ## 2. Choose type for the reading context

  Prefer typefaces that are easy to recognize, and limit the number of families and weights. Avoid highly decorative styles for long passages. A small number of weight or color changes can highlight key information without emphasizing every line.

  ## 3. Adjust line and paragraph spacing

  Tight leading can make text feel crowded, while excessive space can weaken the flow between lines. Separate paragraphs enough to distinguish them without making readers think the topic has changed. Adjust based on how the text reads in context.

  ## 4. Give text and background clear contrast

  Body text should be easy to distinguish from its background. Avoid light text on a light background or placing busy imagery behind essential copy. Brand colors can add emphasis, but important information should remain easy to recognize.

  ## 5. Use whitespace and alignment to organize content

  Whitespace helps separate groups of information; it is not empty space that must be filled. Consistent margins, alignment, and spacing rules make a page feel more organized. Map the relationships between content before choosing columns and visual rhythm.

  ## 6. Check the actual size and device

  A zoomed-in screen preview may not represent how a design reads on a phone, as a printed piece, or from farther away. Before publishing or handing off a design, review headings, body copy, captions, and key details at their intended size. When practical, ask a reader from the intended audience to test it and note any difficult areas.

  ## Layout review checklist

  - Can readers quickly find the title, key points, and next-step information?
  - Are text, backgrounds, and images clearly separated?
  - Are line spacing, paragraph spacing, and alignment consistent?
  - Is essential text still clear at the actual viewing size?

  Reading comfort depends on the content, typeface, medium, and viewing distance; no single set of measurements works for every layout. Reviewing a draft in its real use context is often more effective than adding decoration alone.

排版的目标不只是让页面看起来整齐，还要帮助读者快速找到重点并顺畅阅读。文章、宣传页和活动物料的尺寸与阅读场景不同，下面这些原则可以作为检查起点，再根据实际内容调整。

## 1. 建立清晰的信息层级

先区分主标题、分段标题、正文和补充信息，再用字号、字重、位置或间距表达它们的关系。层级不宜过多；如果所有内容都很醒目，读者反而难以判断先看哪里。

## 2. 选择适合阅读场景的字体

优先选择清晰易辨的字体，并控制字体种类与字重数量。长段正文要避免过度装饰的字形；重点内容可以通过有限的字重或颜色变化突出，而不是每一行都使用强调样式。

## 3. 调整行距与段落间距

行距过紧会让文字显得拥挤，过松则可能削弱句子之间的连贯感。段落之间也应留出足够区分，但不要让间距大到读者误以为内容已经换了主题。可以从实际阅读效果出发逐步调整。

## 4. 让文字与背景有足够区分

正文和背景需要有清晰的明暗对比，避免把浅色文字放在浅色背景上，或让复杂图片干扰阅读。品牌色可以用于强调，但重要信息仍应保持容易辨认。

## 5. 用留白和对齐组织内容

留白能帮助读者区分内容组块，不是需要填满的空缺。统一的边距、对齐线和间隔规则，也能让页面更有秩序。排版前先梳理内容关系，再决定分栏和视觉节奏。

## 6. 在实际尺寸与设备上检查

屏幕上的放大预览不一定能反映手机、印刷成品或远距离观看时的效果。发布或交付前，尽量按实际使用尺寸检查标题、正文、图注和关键信息；必要时请目标读者试读并记录难读的位置。

## 排版检查清单

- 读者能否快速找到标题、重点和下一步信息？
- 正文、背景和图片之间是否有清晰区分？
- 行距、段落间距和对齐方式是否保持一致？
- 缩小到实际使用尺寸后，重要文字是否仍清楚可读？

视觉舒适度会受到内容长度、字体、媒介和观看距离影响，没有一套数值适用于所有版面。完成初稿后从读者的实际使用场景检查，通常比单纯增加装饰更有效。
