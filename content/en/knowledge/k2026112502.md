Industrial safety signs are part of how a facility communicates risk. They mark the hazards people cannot see, state the protective equipment a zone requires, and show the way out when something goes wrong. Because they are bought in sets — often dozens or hundreds of types across one site — factory safety signs and warehouse safety signs are a natural fit for custom manufacturing: produced to your designs, sizes and colours in one coordinated run. This guide covers the common types of industrial safety signs, the material choices behind them, and how to specify a custom order accurately.

## 1. Common types of industrial safety signs

Industrial safety signs are usually organised by the kind of message they carry:

- **Warning signs** — alert people to a hazard: electricity, forklifts, hot surfaces, moving machinery
- **Danger signs** — mark hazards of the most serious kind, where the consequence of ignoring the sign is severe
- **Caution signs** — flag situations that call for care, such as slippery floors or low headroom
- **Prohibition signs** — state what must not be done: no smoking, no entry, no naked flames
- **Mandatory signs** — state what must be done or worn, including most PPE safety signs
- **Fire safety signs** — identify fire equipment, extinguishers, hoses and alarm points
- **Emergency signs** — Emergency Exit signs, escape routes, first aid and assembly points
- **Equipment and warehouse safety signs** — machine-specific instructions, racking and aisle warnings, loading dock rules

Most facilities need a mix of all eight. The practical starting point is a site walk-through or hazard list: each hazard, rule and emergency facility becomes a line in a sign schedule, which then drives the order.

## 2. Warning, Danger and Prohibition signs

Warning, Danger and Caution signs form a severity ladder, and using them consistently matters. If every sign says Danger, none of them do. Reserve Danger for the small number of locations where the risk is immediate and serious; use Warning for hazards that could cause injury; use Caution for lower-level risks and housekeeping hazards. Industrial warning signs work through a combination of a recognisable pictogram, a short headline and, where needed, a line of supplementary text. Keep wording short — a sign that must be read in two seconds cannot carry a paragraph.

Prohibition signs depend on instant recognition: the prohibited action shown in the pictogram must be unambiguous, because these signs are often read by visitors and contractors who do not know the site. When you order custom safety signs, supply the pictograms or reference designs you want followed, and check proofs carefully — a subtly wrong symbol is worse than a plain text sign.

## 3. PPE and Mandatory safety signs

Mandatory signs state required behaviour, and PPE signs are their most common form: eye protection, helmets, gloves, hi-vis clothing, hearing protection, respiratory protection. They belong at zone boundaries — the door, gate or line marking where the requirement starts — not scattered inside the zone where they arrive too late. Where several items of PPE are required, a combined multi-message sign at the entry point is usually clearer than four separate boards.

Mandatory signs also cover actions such as switching off equipment, using guards, washing hands or keeping fire doors shut. For sites with an international workforce, pictogram-led designs with minimal text, or text in the languages your workforce reads, reduce misunderstanding; multilingual wording can be set exactly as you supply it when signs are custom manufactured.

## 4. Fire safety and emergency signs

Fire safety signs identify the equipment and actions needed in the first minutes of an incident: extinguisher and hose reel locations, alarm call points, fire action notices and assembly points. Emergency Exit signs and escape route signs must form a continuous chain — from any point in the building, the next sign should be visible — so plan them as a route, not as individual boards.

These are also the signs most likely to be needed in the dark or in smoke. Consider photoluminescent or reflective face options for exit and fire equipment signs, and confirm the specific face material and its properties with your supplier for your order rather than assuming performance from a product name. Emergency signage is also an area where local building and fire rules often prescribe exact designs; supply the designs or references you are required to follow, and have them reproduced as specified.

## 5. Metal vs. plastic safety signs

Metal safety signs — most commonly aluminum — are flat, rigid, do not rust, and take printed and reflective faces well. They suit long-term installation, outdoor areas, and anywhere the sign must stay presentable for years. Steel options add weight and rigidity for large boards or high-impact locations.

Rigid plastic signs are lighter and cheaper, easy to fix with adhesive or screws, and perfectly adequate indoors; some plastics, however, can yellow or become brittle under prolonged UV exposure, and thin plastics flex on large sizes. Self-adhesive labels serve small equipment-level messages but are not a substitute for rigid signs in traffic areas. As with any sign order, the decision should follow the environment: indoor or outdoor, exposure, size, fixing method and expected life. State these in your enquiry and have the material and thickness confirmed in the quotation — "metal safety signs" alone is not a specification.

## 6. Reflective safety sign options

Reflective faces return headlight and torch light to the viewer, which matters for signs read by vehicle drivers: forklift routes, loading docks, yard crossings, gate areas and car park interfaces. Reflective industrial safety signs are also worth considering for emergency and fire equipment signs in areas that may lose lighting. Indoor signs under constant lighting usually gain nothing from a reflective face, and the cost is better spent on size or material. Reflective films are supplied in different grades; the grade and film type should be confirmed with your supplier for the order, and any specific performance requirement you must meet should be stated in the enquiry so it can be checked explicitly.

## 7. Custom industrial safety sign manufacturing

Custom manufacturing follows a simple sequence. First, the sign schedule: types, wording, pictograms, sizes and quantities, usually built from your hazard assessment or existing sign list. Second, artwork: signs are either produced directly from your print-ready files or set up from your brief and reference designs, with proofs returned for your confirmation — this is the stage to verify symbols, colours and spelling. Third, production: printing, face application, cutting and drilling to the confirmed specification. Fourth, checking and packing: finished signs are photographed before dispatch on export orders, and packed flat with protective film, sorted by type where requested.

Custom production is what makes mixed orders practical: a whole facility's warning, prohibition, PPE, fire and warehouse safety signs produced together, in matching materials and finishes, instead of accumulated from different sources over years.

## 8. How to select industrial safety sign suppliers

Choose a safety signage manufacturer who asks about your environment before quoting a material; who works from your designs and returns proofs; who can produce the full range of types in one run so colours and quality stay consistent; and who packs and documents export orders properly. Be cautious with any supplier who promises that a sign design complies with your local regulations without seeing the requirement — responsibility for confirming that sign designs meet the rules at the installation site rests with the buyer, and a good supplier will say so plainly.

To request a quotation, prepare: the sign types and wording, symbols or reference designs, finished sizes and quantities per type, preferred material and reflective or non-reflective face, mounting method, and delivery destination.
