A printed fence around a construction site has two jobs: carry the message and stay standing. Solid PVC banner does the first job well and the second badly. PVC mesh exists for exactly this reason — it is a banner material with holes in it, so wind passes through instead of pushing the fence over. This guide explains how mesh works, how it differs from solid banner, and what to specify so the fence you order survives the site it is printed for.

## 1. What PVC mesh is

PVC mesh is a woven or coated polyester mesh with a PVC layer carrying the print. Unlike solid banner, its surface is perforated: a proportion of the material is open holes. From a distance it reads as a solid printed graphic; up close you can see through it. That openness is the whole point — it is what lets a large printed panel behave safely on an exposed fence.

Typical characteristics:

- Wind passes through the panel instead of loading it like a sail, so it suits long fence runs and exposed sites
- Lighter in handling than a solid banner of the same size, and easier to fix and tension
- Print is visible from the front; from behind, the image is fainter and the view through is partly open
- The open structure means fine detail and small text do not reproduce as crisply as on solid banner — large logos, bold headlines and simple graphics work best
- Made for outdoor use, but expected service life depends on exposure, fixing and how often the panel is moved and re-used — confirm it for the actual site conditions before ordering

Best for: construction site hoarding and fence wraps, scaffolding wraps, sports ground and event fencing, car park and boundary fencing, and any long outdoor run where wind load is the first concern.

## 2. Why a fence needs mesh, not solid banner

A fence panel is a large flat surface standing in open air. A solid banner catches the wind across its whole area, and the load goes straight into the fence, the posts and the fixings. On a long run that load adds up fast, and the failure is usually not the print — it is a torn eyelet, a pulled fixing, or a section of fence leaning or coming down.

Mesh changes the calculation: because part of the panel is open, much less wind force reaches the fence. Site managers and fencing contractors therefore commonly specify a permeable material for fence and scaffold applications, and in many cases a solid banner is simply not accepted for a long exposed run. The rule to remember is simple — if the panel will live outdoors on a fence, start from mesh and justify solid banner, not the other way around.

## 3. Mesh vs solid banner at a glance

- Wind behaviour — mesh: wind passes through, low load on the fence. Solid banner: catches the wind, high load.
- Print sharpness — mesh: bold graphics read well, fine detail softens. Solid banner: sharper detail and stronger colour.
- Best use — mesh: fences, scaffolds, long outdoor runs. Solid banner: short-term displays, walls and sheltered positions where wind is not a factor.
- Fixing — mesh: lighter to handle and tension; fixings still matter and must be spaced to suit the run. Solid banner: heavier, and needs stronger fixing on any exposed position.
- Relative cost — mesh is a printed finished product like banner, priced per square metre on the same basis; the saving is not in the material, it is in avoiding fence damage and reprints after wind failure.

## 4. How to choose mesh for a fence job

**Start with the site, not the material.** How long is the fence run, how tall is it, how exposed is the site, and how long will the panel stay up? A short sheltered run and a long exposed highway fence are different jobs, and the mesh, finishing and fixing should follow the harder of the two.

**Balance print against openness.** A more open mesh lets more wind through but carries less print surface, so the image is softer. A tighter mesh prints better but loads the fence more. For fence work the usual answer is to favour openness and keep the artwork bold — large logos, short headlines, high-contrast colours — rather than chasing photographic detail the material cannot hold.

**Confirm the weight and openness with the supplier.** Mesh is sold by weight and by how open it is, and both vary between manufacturers. Do not order on a product name alone: ask for the weight and openness of the actual material being quoted, and check that the sample or the printed proof matches it.

**Choose the finishing for how the panel is fixed.** Common options are a welded hem with eyelets at regular intervals, pole pockets for fence rails, or reinforced edges for cable ties. Tell the supplier how the panel will be fixed and how often it will be taken down and re-used — the finishing should be specified for that, not chosen as an afterthought.

**Check the print method and the back.** Mesh is usually printed on one side. Confirm whether the back will show a faint reverse image, whether the print is expected to be read from one side only, and whether any lamination or coating is included — and if so, what it is for.

## 5. What to put in your enquiry

A mesh enquiry that can be quoted without three rounds of questions states: fence length and height (or panel sizes), whether the site is exposed or sheltered, the artwork (and whether it is mostly bold graphics or fine detail), the fixing method, the quantity, and the delivery destination and timing. For Australian buyers, sea freight to main Australian ports typically takes around 40 days in transit, so allow for that in the project schedule and confirm the production time with the quotation.

## 6. When not to use mesh

Do not use mesh where the graphic must be read at close range with fine detail — a retail window, an exhibition panel or a small sign. Do not use it where the back of the panel will be seen close up and a clean reverse matters. And do not use it as a substitute for a rigid board: mesh is a flexible fence material, not a sign panel. For those jobs, solid banner, self-adhesive vinyl or a rigid board is the right material, and the choice should be made on the use, not on the price per square metre.

Specify the material by its use — "printed PVC mesh for a construction fence, fixed with eyelets, exposed site" — rather than by a product name alone, and confirm weight, openness, print method, finishing and fixing before the quotation is prepared. If the site is exposed or the run is long, describe the conditions in your enquiry so the material can be checked against the actual job.
