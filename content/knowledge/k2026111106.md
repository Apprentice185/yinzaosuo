body_en: |
  When a banner fails outdoors, it is rarely because the print was bad. It is usually because the weight was wrong: too light and the wind tears it out of its eyelets; too heavy and you pay extra freight and wrestle it into place with two people. Weight — GSM — is the first question in choosing a banner, and getting it right makes everything after it easier.

  ## What GSM actually means

  GSM is grams per square metre: a 340 GSM material weighs 340 grams per square metre. Australian and North American suppliers often use ounces per square yard for the same thing. Roughly:

  | Weight | Approx. ounces | What the market usually calls it |
  |---|---|---|
  | 340 GSM | approx. 10 oz | Light / standard banner |
  | 440 GSM | approx. 13 oz | General-purpose outdoor banner |
  | 510 GSM | approx. 15 oz | Heavy / double-sided banner |

  Two warnings before that table misleads anyone:

  - **Weight is not thickness, and it is not strength.** Two 440 GSM banners can tear very differently, because the base fabric's denier (how coarse the polyester yarn is) and the coating process differ. GSM is the starting point for selection, not the only strength figure.
  - **A weight number usually comes with a construction.** 340, 440 and 510 are common grades of PVC-coated banner, but the same weight also comes as frontlit, backlit or blockout — see the third section. Quoting weight without construction is only half the specification.

  ## The short answer

  - **340 GSM**: indoors, short-term, sheltered spots. Trade shows, in-store promotions, event banners that come down after a few weeks. Light, easy to roll up, cheap to ship.
  - **440 GSM**: the general outdoor grade. Shopfronts, roadside promotions, outdoor events, a few months outdoors. Most banners should be this grade.
  - **510 GSM**: long-term, large-format, double-sided. Long-life outdoor advertising, large facade banners, anything printed both sides.
  - **Windy sites don't need a heavier banner — they need mesh.** A solid banner is a sail, and a heavier one catches more wind, not less. For exposed fencing and scaffolding, the right answer is PVC mesh (see [article #2](/articles/pvc-mesh-construction-fencing-guide/)), not swapping 440 for 510.

  ## 1. The three grades side by side: 340 / 440 / 510

  | Weight | Position | Suits | Watch out for |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 340 GSM (approx. 10 oz) | Light | Indoor displays, trade shows, short promotions, sheltered in-store banners | Not for long outdoor use; short life in wind and sun |
  | 440 GSM (approx. 13 oz) | General outdoor | Shopfronts, roadside banners, outdoor events, months outdoors | The default choice for most banners; large formats and windy spots need proper finishing |
  | 510 GSM (approx. 15 oz) | Heavy | Long-term outdoor advertising, large facade banners, double-sided banners, demanding spots near lightboxes | Heavier, dearer, harder to handle and store; wasted on short-term jobs |

  Choose by asking in this order: where will it hang (indoors or out) → how long (weeks or years) → how big (a small banner or a whole wall) → how windy is it. Those four answers settle the grade.

  ## 2. Beyond weight: construction matters more than the number

  The same 440 GSM means different things in different constructions. State construction and weight together when ordering:

  - **Frontlit**: the standard banner — light falls on the front and the image is viewed directly. Shopfronts, event banners, hoarding graphics: this is the default.
  - **Backlit**: a translucent material lit from behind, so it stays readable at night. Lightbox advertising and night-time signage need it; a frontlit banner in a lightbox stays dark because the light cannot get through.
  - **Blockout**: a dark core layer between two PVC faces, so neither side shows through and both sides can carry different artwork. Double-sided banners, roadside double-sided signs, and any spot with strong light behind the banner need it; print both sides on ordinary single-face material and the back artwork ghosts through to the front.

  One case is easy to miss: **what sits behind the banner.** A banner in front of a glass facade, or in front of another shop's sign, looks terrible if light and lettering bleed through from behind. That calls for blockout construction, not a heavier weight.

  ## 3. Wind: a banner's worst enemy is not the sun, it is the wind

  A solid banner outdoors is a sail. Wind damage depends on area and fixings, not on weight alone:

  - **Heavier catches more wind.** A large 510 banner in a gale takes far more load than a small 340 banner. Fighting wind with weight alone works in the wrong direction.
  - **Windy sites take mesh.** Perforated PVC mesh lets wind pass through, so the same size carries far less load. Fencing, scaffolding and long banners on open ground should be mesh — see [article #2](/articles/pvc-mesh-construction-fencing-guide/) for how to choose it.
  - **Finishing matters as much as weight.** Hemming, eyelet spacing, rope finishing and pole pockets decide whether a banner tears out of its fixings. Our banner product offers hemming, eyelets, rope finishing and pole pockets (see the [banner product page](/products/ys-pvc-banner/)); settle the installation when ordering — what it hangs from, how it is fixed, and any eyelet spacing you need.
  - **Leave a little slack.** A banner pulled drum-tight has no give when the wind hits, so the eyelets take the full load; a little movement in the fabric is less likely to tear.

  ## 4. Outdoor life: don't treat weight as a life promise

  The general reference Australian trade sources give is about 2–3 years outdoors for a sound solid PVC banner. That is a general reference, not a promise — real life depends on sun exposure, installation, how often it is taken down and put back, and whether it is cleaned. The same roll can last twice as long in a shaded doorway as it does facing the afternoon sun.

  Two things that decide life but never appear on a weight table:

  1. **Ink and lamination.** Outdoor banner inks need UV resistance; long outdoor runs can be laminated for protection — confirmed per order.
  2. **How it is put away.** A banner folded into hard creases starts its next outing with a crack waiting to happen. Roll it for storage; don't fold and stuff it.

  ## Common mistakes

  1. **Heavier is better.** A 510 for a short indoor event buys unneeded life with extra freight and install cost.
  2. **Weight without construction.** A frontlit banner in a lightbox, or single-face material printed double-sided, fails whatever the weight.
  3. **Adding weight for wind.** Windy sites need mesh, or smaller panels with more fixing points.
  4. **510 for a small banner.** A one- or two-metre banner is fine on 440; 510's stiffness makes a small banner hard to hang flat.
  5. **Forgetting the back.** Light or another sign behind the banner — confirm blockout first.
  6. **Eyelet spacing left to chance.** On a large banner, eyelets set too far apart let the wind start the tear at the eyelet; spacing belongs in the order, sized to the banner and the wind.

  ## When a solid banner is the wrong choice

  - **Long-term exposure to strong wind on fencing and scaffolding** — use mesh, see [article #2](/articles/pvc-mesh-construction-fencing-guide/).
  - **A lightbox that needs to glow** — use backlit material, not thicker frontlit.
  - **Both sides printed** — use blockout or a double-sided construction; single-face material shows through.
  - **Very large formats changed often** — confirm width and seaming first, before printing, not after.

  ## What to put in an order

  A usable order states: finished width and height, quantity, artwork files, where it hangs (indoors, outdoors, how windy), how long it must last, single- or double-sided, whether it is for a lightbox, and the finishing (hemming, eyelet spacing, rope, pole pockets). 一张横幅挂出去之后坏掉，很少是因为印得不好。多半是克重选错了：太轻的横幅风一吹就从鸡眼扣处撕开，太重的横幅白白多花运费，两个人抬着安装都费劲。克重（gsm）就是选横幅的第一道题，答对了，后面的事都顺。

## 先说克重是什么

克重用 gsm 计：每平方米的材料重多少克。340 gsm 就是一平方米重 340 克。澳洲和北美同行常用盎司（oz/yd²）说同一个东西，换算关系大致是：

| 克重 | 约合盎司 | 市场上的常见叫法 |
|---|---|---|
| 340 gsm | 约 10 oz | 轻量/标准横幅 |
| 440 gsm | 约 13 oz | 户外通用横幅 |
| 510 gsm | 约 15 oz | 加重/双面横幅 |

有两点要先说清楚，不然这个数字会骗人：

- **克重不等于厚度，也不等于强度。** 同样 440 gsm 的横幅，基布的旦尼尔（涤纶纱的粗细）和涂层工艺不同，抗撕裂的能力可以差很多。克重是选型的起点，不是唯一的强度指标。
- **克重数字后面常跟着结构。** 340、440、510 都是 PVC 涂层横幅的常见档；同样克重的材料，还分 frontlit（正面受光）、backlit（灯箱透光）、blockout（中间夹黑层、双面不透）——见第三节。只报克重不报结构，等于只说了半句话。

## 先给结论

- **340 gsm**：室内、短期、遮风的地方。展会、店内促销、用了几周就换的活动横幅，选它。轻、好收、运费便宜。
- **440 gsm**：户外通用档。店招、路边促销、几个月的户外活动，大多数横幅选这一档就够了。
- **510 gsm**：长期、大幅面、双面。长期户外广告牌、大型立面横幅、双面都要印的横幅，选它。
- **风大的地方别加克重，换网布。** 实心横幅越重、兜风越狠；暴露在风里的围挡和脚手架，正确做法是换 PVC 网布（见[第二篇](/articles/pvc-mesh-construction-fencing-guide/)），不是把 440 换成 510。

## 一、三档对照：340 / 440 / 510

| 克重 | 定位 | 适合 | 注意 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 340 gsm（约 10 oz） | 轻量 | 室内展示、展会、短期促销、遮风的店内横幅 | 不适合长期户外；风吹日晒下寿命短 |
| 440 gsm（约 13 oz） | 户外通用 | 店招、路边横幅、户外活动、几个月的户外投放 | 大多数横幅的默认选择；大幅面或大风处要配好后道 |
| 510 gsm（约 15 oz） | 加重 | 长期户外广告、大型立面横幅、双面横幅、灯箱附近的高要求场合 | 更重、更贵、更难收放；短期活动用它是浪费 |

选的时候按这个顺序问自己：挂在哪（室内还是户外）→ 挂多久（几周还是几年）→ 多大（小横幅还是整面墙）→ 风大不大。四个答案一出来，档位基本就定了。

## 二、克重之外：结构比数字更重要

同样是 440 gsm，结构不同，用途完全不同。下单时要把结构和克重一起说清楚：

- **Frontlit（正面受光）**：最常见的横幅，光从前面打过来，画面直接看。店招、活动横幅、围挡画面，默认就是它。
- **Backlit（背光/灯箱）**：半透光的材料，灯从后面打亮，晚上也看得清。灯箱广告、夜间招牌要用它；拿 frontlit 去做灯箱，灯光透不过来，画面是暗的。
- **Blockout（遮光/双面）**：中间夹了一层深色芯，两面互不透光，可以双面印不同的画面。双面横幅、路边双面立牌、背后有强光的场合要用它；普通单面料双面印，背面的字会透到前面来。

还有一种情况容易被忽略：**横幅背面是什么。** 横幅挂在玻璃幕墙前面、或者背后就是另一家店的招牌，背面透光透字会很难看。这种时候要的不是更重的克重，而是 blockout 结构。

## 三、风：横幅最大的敌人不是太阳，是风

实心横幅挂在户外，本质上是一面帆。风的破坏力不看克重，看面积和固定方式：

- **克重越重，兜风越狠。** 一张 510 的大幅面横幅在风里受的力，比一张 340 的小横幅大得多。只靠加克重抗风，是反方向。
- **风大的地方换网布。** PVC 网布带孔，风能穿过去，同样尺寸下受的风力小得多。围挡、脚手架、空旷场地的长横幅，网布才是正确选择，具体怎么选见[第二篇](/articles/pvc-mesh-construction-fencing-guide/)。
- **后道和克重一样重要。** 包边、鸡眼扣的间距、加绳、穿杆袋——这些决定横幅会不会从固定点撕开。我们的横幅产品支持包边、打鸡眼扣、加绳和穿杆袋（见[横幅产品页](/products/ys-pvc-banner/)），下单时把安装方式一起定下来：挂在什么上、用什么固定、鸡眼扣间距有没有要求。
- **留一点松量。** 横幅绷得太紧，风一来没有缓冲，全靠鸡眼扣硬扛；适当留一点活动量，反而不容易撕。

## 四、户外寿命：别把克重当寿命承诺

澳洲同行给的一般参考是：质量合格的实心 PVC 横幅，户外寿命约 2–3 年。这是一般参考，不是承诺——实际寿命取决于日晒强度、安装方式、收放次数和有没有定期清洁。同一卷材料，遮阴门口的横幅和下午晒太阳的横幅，寿命可以差一倍。

真正决定寿命的还有两件事，克重表上看不出来：

1. **油墨和覆膜。** 户外横幅的油墨要耐 UV；长期户外使用可以覆膜保护，具体做法按订单确认。
2. **收起来的时候。** 横幅收卷时折出死褶，下次展开就是一道裂纹的起点。卷起来存放，别对折硬塞。

## 常见误区

1. **越重越好。** 短期室内活动用 510，是拿运费和安装成本买不需要的寿命。
2. **只看克重，不看结构。** 灯箱用了 frontlit、双面用了单面料，克重再对也没用。
3. **大风处加克重。** 风大的地方要换网布，或者缩小单幅面积、增加固定点。
4. **小横幅也上 510。** 一两米的小横幅，440 已经绰绰有余；510 的硬挺反而让小横幅不好挂平。
5. **忘了问背面。** 背后有光、背后是另一块招牌，先确认要不要 blockout。
6. **鸡眼扣间距随手定。** 大幅面横幅的鸡眼扣太稀，风一来先从扣眼处撕起；间距要按尺寸和风况定，下单时写进需求里。

## 什么时候不该用实心横幅

- **长期暴露在大风里的围挡和脚手架**——用网布，见[第二篇](/articles/pvc-mesh-construction-fencing-guide/)。
- **要透光的灯箱**——用 backlit 材料，不是加厚的 frontlit。
- **双面都要印**——用 blockout 或双面结构，单面料会透字。
- **超大幅面又要经常换画面**——先确认幅宽和拼接方式，别等印完了才发现要接缝。

## 下单时怎么说明需求

一份能用的需求包括：成品宽度和高度、数量、画面文件、挂在哪（室内、户外、风大不大）、用多久、单面还是双面、是不是灯箱、后道加工（包边、鸡眼扣间距、加绳、穿杆袋）。克重、幅宽、起订量和价格，按每单的实际规格逐项确认——通过[横幅产品页](/products/ys-pvc-banner/)把需求发过来，就可以逐项定下来。
